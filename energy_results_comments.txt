ENERGY CONSUMPTION FORECASTING - RESULTS EVALUATION & COMMENTS

1. PERFORMANCE METRICS OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The performance of the three models tested on the energy forecasting dataset 
is summarized below (sorted by Root Mean Squared Error - RMSE):

Model               | MAE        | RMSE       | R2 Score
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. XGBoost          | 134.2096   | 168.1722   | 0.955992
2. MLP Regressor    | 148.3047   | 185.7097   | 0.946335
3. Linear Regression| 149.1504   | 187.2748   | 0.945426


2. MODEL-SPECIFIC COMMENTS & ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
### A. XGBoost (Best Performer)
* Metric Insights: Achieved the lowest error (MAE ~134.2 MW, RMSE ~168.2 MW) and 
  the highest variance explanation (R2 ≈ 95.6%).
* Strengths: Gradient boosted trees excel at learning complex interaction boundaries
  and capturing non-linear relationships without needing extensive feature 
  transformations. It handles time features and lag attributes exceptionally well, 
  avoiding overfitting through its ensemble structure and default/configured 
  regularization parameters.

### B. MLP Regressor (Multi-Layer Perceptron / Neural Network)
* Metric Insights: MAE ~148.3 MW, RMSE ~185.7 MW, R2 ≈ 94.6%.
* Analysis: The Neural Network model placed second, performing slightly better 
  than the baseline Linear Regression but lagging behind XGBoost. While MLPs are 
  powerful universal approximators, tabular time series data with localized shifted 
  lag metrics often require more intensive tuning (e.g., specific batch sizes, 
  learning rates, architectures, or more epochs) to outperform tree-based methods.

### C. Linear Regression (Baseline)
* Metric Insights: MAE ~149.1 MW, RMSE ~187.3 MW, R2 ≈ 94.5%.
* Analysis: For a simple, un-regularized baseline model, Linear Regression 
  performs surprisingly well here. This is primarily because the feature 
  engineering pipeline explicitly included direct lag terms (`lag_24h` and `lag_7d`). 
  Because energy consumption is highly autoregressive (today looks a lot like 
  yesterday and last week), a linear combination of these lags already captures 
  the majority of the signal.


3. KEY TAKEAWAYS & PIPELINE OBSERVATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Lag Features are King: The strong performance across all models (all R2 scores 
  exceeding 94%) indicates that the engineered lag variables (`lag_24h` and 
  `lag_7d`) provide a strong autoregressive signal that linear, tree-based, 
  and neural network structures can easily exploit.
* Why XGBoost Won: XGBoost successfully captures the residual conditional anomalies 
  and non-linear interactions between the categorical time indicators (hour, 
  day of week, month) and the continuous lag variables, which Linear Regression 
  cannot do without explicit interaction terms.
* Recommendations for Next Steps:
  1. Feature expansion: Incorporate rolling averages (e.g., 6-hour or 24-hour window 
     means/stds) to smooth out stochastic hourly noise.
  2. Hyperparameter Optimization: Fine-tune the MLP Regressor layer sizes or try 
     a learning rate scheduler to close the gap with XGBoost.
